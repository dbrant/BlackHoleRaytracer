What's this?

What you're seeing, physically

  • This simulation shows the gravitational lensing effects caused by a black hole, done using real-time ray tracing with an actual field equation that models a Schwarzschild black hole (the simplest possible black hole: non-rotating, non-charged, spherically symmetric).
  • Strictly speaking, what you see is an instantaneous snapshot of how light rays are bent by the presence of the black hole. Even though you can move around and change the viewpoint, it should not be interpreted as what you would actually experience at this distance from a real black hole, with time flowing normally. If you were actually next to a black hole at this distance, and we start time moving forward, you would need to accelerate like crazy to hold your position relative to the black hole. This immense acceleration would create extreme relativistic effects like aberration, blueshift, time dilation, and tidal forces. This is currently outside the scope of this visualization.
  • Nevertheless, the simulation provides an accurate depiction of how light is bent by the black hole's gravity, and can be useful for building intuition for how gravity affects light at the extremes.

What you can do

  • Point and drag to change the viewpoint around the black hole. Pinch or scroll to zoom.
  • Play around with turning on the other checkboxes to render various toy objects around the black hole, including stars, planets, and reflective/transparent spheres. These objects are totally unphysical, but they help demonstrate that this is a real raytracer.
  • Rotate the viewpoint until an object is directly behind the black hole to see an Einstein ring.
  • Experiment with the Curvature slider to see how the strength of the curvature affects the bending of light around the black hole.
